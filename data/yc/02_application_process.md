# How to apply to Y Combinator

YC runs two batches a year:
- **Winter batch**: January through March; application window opens around
  August/September of the prior year.
- **Summer batch**: June through August; application window opens around
  February/March of the same year.

Since 2024 YC has also experimented with **Spring** and **Fall** batches to
accommodate founder demand.

## The application
The application is a web form completed by the founding team. It typically
asks for:
1. **Company description** in 50 characters or less.
2. **What does your company do?** — concrete, jargon-free, one paragraph.
3. **Founders' background** — what have you built, shipped, or founded
   previously? Include relevant URLs (LinkedIn, GitHub, prior startups).
4. **Progress so far** — users, revenue, signed letters of intent, GMV.
5. **Why now?** — what changed in the world that makes this idea timely?
6. **Why this team?** — domain expertise, prior collaboration, complementary
   skills.
7. A short **video (~60s)** with each cofounder on camera.

## What YC partners look for
YC partners often say they bet on **founders**, not ideas. They look for:
- **Clarity of thought** in the written answers.
- **Speed of iteration** — evidence the team ships fast.
- **Ramen profitability mindset** or a clear path to it.
- **Market insight** — a non-obvious thesis about the customer or market.
- Strong **cofounder relationship** (long-standing co-workers preferred over
  cofounders who met recently).

## Interview
Shortlisted teams are invited to a **10-minute interview** with YC partners.
Key advice from PG and others:
- Answer the question that was asked, briefly.
- Don't pitch — partners have already read the application.
- Be ready to explain the business in one sentence.
- It is fine to say "I don't know."

## After acceptance
Founders sign the standard YC deal (see [[01-about-yc]] for the $500k SAFE
structure) and join the batch. Accepted founders move to the Bay Area for the
in-person portion of the program.
